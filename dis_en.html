Introduction

Hello, I’m Chamu. I’d like to share my experience visiting Tokyo Disneyland, a place I love. I have a visual impairment and food allergies. I hope the support I received from cast members may help others who are planning a visit.

About this page： This page combines my personal experience with information from official guidance. Services and operating procedures can change, so please check the official information before your visit.

Disability Access Service (DAS)

DAS is intended to reduce the burden for eligible guests who have difficulty waiting in the regular queue at participating attractions. Ask a cast member at the facility about eligibility and use.

Important points

When the guest has difficulty waiting in the queue for a long time, the group may wait somewhere outside the queue. DAS does not shorten the waiting time, and the group cannot use another participating attraction while waiting.

Examples of accepted documentation

Examples include a physical disability certificate, mental disability certificate, rehabilitation certificate, atomic bomb survivor’s certificate, war-injury certificate, and certain disability-service or designated intractable-disease certificates. Guests visiting from outside Japan should bring disability documentation issued under the laws of their country of residence. Check the official page for the full current list.

Pregnant guests, older guests, and guests temporarily unable to wait in line because of an injury or illness may be directed to a different service, such as the Meet-Up Service, depending on their circumstances. This is separate from DAS; ask a cast member for guidance.

How to ask for DAS

  1. Tell a cast member at a participating facility that you would like to ask about DAS.
  2. Present the guest’s eligible documentation (the original or a supported disability ID app) and park tickets for everyone in the group.
  3. Follow the cast member’s instructions to register. A photo may be taken to verify the guest’s identity.
  4. When using the service, present the park ticket used for registration and follow the cast member’s instructions.

Bring the required documentation and park tickets. If you plan to use your phone, make sure it is charged. Procedures may change, so follow the cast members’ instructions on the day.

Restaurants and food allergies

I have egg and seafood allergies, so I sometimes worry about which menu items I can eat. During my visit, cast members helped me check information and made the menu easier for me to understand. Their support made a real difference.

Important safety note Allergy information and available options vary by restaurant and item. For your safety, tell a cast member about your allergies before ordering and check the official food-allergy guidance. My experience does not guarantee that every restaurant can provide the same support.

Mobile Order may not allow ingredient removal or changes at the time of ordering. In some cases, you may need to speak with a cast member at the restaurant after placing the order. Check the official FAQ in advance. Payment options may also vary by service and location.

Tickets for guests with disabilities

Tokyo Disney Resort offers a 1-Day Passport for guests with disabilities. It can be purchased by an eligible guest and one accompanying guest. Each person needs their own ticket.

Conclusion
